# MSc Thesis — SPX Volatility-Surface Risk

**Author:** Alireza Moslemi Haghighi  
**Project lineage:** MSc thesis core and subsequent research extensions  
**Research sample:** SPX options, 3 January 2005–31 December 2021  
**Research data:** WRDS OptionMetrics IvyDB US  
**Base paper:** Zamani, Moslemi Haghighi, and Arian, *Temporal Volatility Surface Projection: Parametric Surface Projection Method for Derivatives Portfolio Risk Management*, arXiv:2311.14985

## Executive summary

The project estimates option-portfolio market risk while allowing the complete implied-volatility surface—not a single volatility input—to move jointly across forward moneyness and maturity. The MSc thesis develops the Parametric Surface Projection (PSP) framework: represent the current surface, project complete historical surface movements, combine them with a future underlying-price state, and reprice the option portfolio.

The subsequent research extension builds a reproducible 2005–2021 WRDS pipeline and adds a fixed 45-node surface grid, multiple reconstruction methods, rolling covariance and correlation PCA, a Gaussian benchmark, daily Heston calibration, physical-versus-risk-neutral Heston experiments, fixed-strike nonlinear revaluation, and validation at 90%, 95%, and 99%.

In the controlled surface-shock experiment, PSP, covariance PCA, correlation PCA, and Heston-HS pass joint conditional coverage at all three levels; the full-grid Gaussian PWG benchmark rejects at all three levels. In the separate full-revaluation experiment, Heston-MC-P is nearly exact at 95% but rejects at 90% and 99%. Heston-MC-Q is a pricing-measure sensitivity rather than a physical VaR law and rejects joint coverage throughout.

The two P&L experiments answer different questions and are not pooled. The first isolates surface risk through a disclosed vega map. The second includes fixed strikes, spot movement, convexity, maturity roll-down, dividends, financing, and a daily delta hedge.

## 1. Research objective

The research object is the SPX implied-volatility surface

\[
\sigma_t(m,T), \qquad m=F/K,
\]

where `m` is forward moneyness and `T` is time to maturity. The project studies the distribution of portfolio P&L generated by movements of this entire surface and, in the full-revaluation experiment, by joint movements in spot and variance.

A smooth fit is not itself a risk forecast. The project therefore separates:

1. representation of the current option market;
2. specification of a dated physical transition law;
3. portfolio pricing or repricing under the projected state; and
4. out-of-sample validation of VaR and Expected Shortfall.

## 2. Parametric Surface Projection

Let the fitted surface on date `t` be `sigma_t(m,T)`. A historical full-surface shock is

\[
\Delta \sigma_s = \sigma_s-\sigma_{s-1}.
\]

At forecast origin `t`, PSP uses only information available through `t`. It samples a historical complete-surface shock, applies that shock to the current market state, evaluates the projected surface at simulated future moneyness and rolled maturity, and reprices the portfolio.

The operational sequence is:

1. fit the current implied-volatility surface;
2. construct dated historical movements of the complete surface;
3. sample a coherent surface shock with no look-ahead;
4. simulate the underlying-price state;
5. update forward moneyness for fixed strikes;
6. reprice the positions; and
7. compute and validate VaR and Expected Shortfall.

This preserves empirical dependence across surface nodes and retains stress shapes that a single-volatility or independent-node model would discard.

## 3. Data and preprocessing

SPX identifiers are discovered from OptionMetrics security-name history. Annual option tables supply quote date, expiration, option type, strike, bid, offer, volume, open interest, implied volatility, Greeks, and vendor forward. The security-price table supplies the underlying close. The zero curve comes from OptionMetrics `zerocd` and is interpolated by days to expiration; the index dividend yield comes from `idxdvd`.

The long-horizon production filters retain calls with:

- mid-price of at least $1;
- 15–550 days to expiration;
- forward moneyness between 0.70 and 1.30;
- relative spread no greater than 1.0;
- nonnegative open interest;
- positive implied volatility; and
- at least 25 eligible observations per date.

The reporting grid uses moneyness 0.80–1.20 and maturities 30, 60, 90, 180, and 360 days.

## 4. Surface reconstruction

All surface families model log implied volatility on normalized forward moneyness

\[
x=\frac{\log(F/K)}{\sqrt{T}}.
\]

The compared methods are:

- a Dumas-style polynomial;
- a cubic tensor-product B-spline with ridge stabilization;
- a scaled thin-plate radial basis; and
- local Delaunay linear interpolation with nearest fitted fallback.

Every 21st trading date is evaluated through a deterministic date-wise holdout. Each fixed-grid node is checked against the daily convex hull in normalized moneyness and maturity. Unsupported nodes use the nearest fitted observation and remain flagged.

| Method | Median IV RMSE | Mean IV RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Linear | 0.00367 | 0.00437 |
| B-spline | 0.00370 | 0.00419 |
| Thin plate | 0.00553 | 0.00596 |
| Polynomial | 0.01209 | 0.01247 |

Linear interpolation has the lowest median holdout RMSE by a small margin. The B-spline has the lower mean RMSE and provides a stable dense surface for downstream factor analysis. The production choice therefore balances held-out error, smoothness, and PCA compatibility. The long-run out-of-hull grid share is 24.00%.

## 5. PCA and factor interpretation

PCA is re-estimated at every forecast origin using only the rolling historical window. Five covariance components explain 96.71% of daily physical-IV change variation.

| Component | Individual share | Cumulative share |
|---|---:|---:|
| PC1 | 77.03% | 77.03% |
| PC2 | 14.68% | 91.71% |
| PC3 | 2.48% | 94.19% |
| PC4 | 1.52% | 95.71% |

Raw covariance PCA should not automatically be labeled level–slope–curvature because high-variance short-dated wing nodes dominate its geometry. After node-wise standardization, correlation PCA aligns more closely with canonical shapes.

## 6. Heston representation and the P/Q distinction

Under Heston,

\[
\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=(r-q)dt+\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^S,
\]

\[
dv_t=\kappa(\theta-v_t)dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^v,
\qquad d\langle W^S,W^v\rangle_t=\rho\,dt.
\]

Option calibration identifies risk-neutral `Q` parameters. VaR concerns real-world `P` probabilities. The full-revaluation experiment therefore estimates a trailing physical transition for one-day spot and variance scenarios while using the current `Q` pricing function for conditional horizon option values. Heston-MC-Q is retained only as a pricing-measure sensitivity.

The median daily Heston IV RMSE is 1.10%, and the 90th percentile is 1.90%. Only 3.1% of daily fits satisfy the Feller inequality, and `xi` reaches its disclosed upper bound on 32.7% of dates. Daily Heston surfaces are useful structural projections, but individual parameter estimates should not be treated as stable structural truths.

## 7. Scenario laws

The controlled surface experiment compares:

- **PSP:** exponentially weighted historical complete-surface shocks;
- **PCA:** bootstrap of rolling PCA-reconstructed shocks;
- **PWG:** Ledoit–Wolf full-grid Gaussian benchmark;
- **PCA-C:** correlation PCA after node standardization; and
- **Heston-HS:** historical changes of daily Heston-implied surfaces.

The full-revaluation experiment compares:

- **GBM+PSP-FHS:** paired historical spot innovation and complete surface shock;
- **Heston-MC-P:** joint physical spot–variance simulation with conditional `Q` repricing; and
- **Heston-MC-Q:** risk-neutral transition and repricing, reported as a sensitivity.

## 8. Portfolio experiments

### Controlled surface-risk experiment

A disclosed positive exposure vector is normalized to total vega 500,000. Daily realized P&L is the exposure-weighted change of the observed 45-node B-spline surface. This isolates volatility-surface risk and holds non-vega effects outside the target.

### Fixed-strike full revaluation

Strikes are fixed at the forecast origin, maturities roll down by the actual calendar gap, a daily delta hedge and cash account are carried, and every call is repriced one day later. Realized P&L includes spot movement, gamma, vega, theta, dividends, financing, and nonlinear volatility effects. All engines forecast the same 4,027 realized portfolio outcomes.

## 9. VaR and validation

For scenario P&L `Pi` and confidence `c`,

\[
VaR_c=-Q_{1-c}(\Pi),
\]

and Expected Shortfall is the negative average P&L below that quantile. A breach occurs when realized P&L is below `-VaR`.

Validation combines:

- Kupiec unconditional coverage;
- Christoffersen independence;
- joint conditional coverage;
- consistent lower-tail quantile loss;
- HAC Diebold–Mariano tests; and
- Murphy diagrams.

## 10. Controlled surface-risk results

| Model | 90% breaches | 95% breaches | 99% breaches | Joint coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| PSP | 386 | 186 | 41 | Pass at all levels |
| PCA | 402 | 213 | 48 | Pass at all levels |
| PWG | 255 | 155 | 62 | Reject at all levels |
| PCA-C | 410 | 205 | 46 | Pass at all levels |
| Heston-HS | 371 | 186 | 36 | Pass at all levels |

Expected counts are 402.7, 201.4, and 40.3. Heston-HS has the lowest average quantile loss among the five surface-shock engines, but its improvement over PSP is not statistically significant at 5%. The evidence supports Heston-HS as competitive, not universally dominant.

## 11. Full-revaluation results

| Model | 90% breaches | 95% breaches | 99% breaches | Joint coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GBM+PSP-FHS | 382 | 187 | 37 | Pass at 90%; reject at 95% and 99% |
| Heston-MC-P | 333 | 200 | 72 | Pass at 95%; reject at 90% and 99% |
| Heston-MC-Q | 110 | 73 | 45 | Reject at all levels |

Heston-MC-P is essentially exact at 95%: 200 breaches against 201.4 expected, with joint conditional-coverage `p=0.947`. It is conservative at 90% and anti-conservative at 99%. The diffusion captures an intermediate tail but not the most extreme discontinuous or regime-changing losses. Heston-MC-Q is not a physical forecasting law.

## 12. Public browser scope

The accompanying teaching page is not a public interface to WRDS or OptionMetrics. It does not load quote-level observations, fitted historical surfaces, credentials, or licensed caches.

Its displayed surface is generated locally from visible controls. For forward moneyness `m` and maturity `T`, the browser starts from the selected ATM volatility and adds:

- a term component controlled by the term-slope input;
- a skew component proportional to `m-1`;
- a curvature component proportional to `(m-1)^2`; and
- a small deterministic adjustment for the selected browser shape family.

Scenario paths are generated by a seeded pseudo-random generator and stylized Heston-, GBM/FHS-, or PCA-inspired equations. Portfolio P&L is calculated from the disclosed delta, vega, gamma, vanna, hedging, and revaluation assumptions. The browser’s backtest table is a fixed aggregate summary from the empirical research; it does not expose the underlying licensed records.

## 13. Limitations

The research uses a disclosed synthetic exposure shape rather than a live trading portfolio. The B-spline surface enforces positivity and applies hull controls but does not impose global calendar and butterfly no-arbitrage. Heston is a one-factor diffusion without jumps, and its physical variance state is proxied by calibrated option-implied `v0`. At 99%, 2,000 scenarios leave only about 20 draws in the Expected Shortfall tail. Transaction costs, discrete hedge execution, and portfolio import remain outside scope.

## Conclusion

The project connects surface representation, factor compression, scenario generation, option repricing, and formal tail validation in one reproducible framework. Historical complete-surface methods remain strong transparent benchmarks. PCA provides useful compression but requires care in factor interpretation. Heston supplies a disciplined structural representation and a useful joint dynamics experiment, but neither its fit nor its 95% success supports a universal claim about tail adequacy.

The central lesson is methodological: a visually convincing surface is not automatically a valid transition law, a risk-neutral pricing model is not automatically a physical VaR model, and one passing confidence level is not sufficient evidence of reliable tail performance.
